How it works

  1. Pick the changes. Load a progression from the library or build your own chord by chord. Songs run up to 32 bars.
  2. Add melody. Pick a block on the left and click the grid where its first note goes, draw notes by hand, or record from your computer keys or a MIDI keyboard.
  3. Shape it. Select notes and use the tools above the grid to trim a run, drop every other note, slow it down, or delete notes and close the gap.
  4. Check the colors. Every note is graded against the chord under it. Hover a note to read why.

Note colors

  • Green, chord tone. Safe to land on and hold.
  • White, scale tone. Fits the chord's scale. Good for moving through.
  • Blue, blue note. A bluesy rub that sounds intentional.
  • Dashed, passing tone. Outside or rubbing, but short and off the beat, so it passes.
  • Yellow, avoid note. A half step above a chord tone. Fine when it moves on, harsh when held.
  • Orange, outside. Not in the chord's scale. Check that you meant it.
  • Red, clash. Grinds a half step against a chord tone on a strong beat or held.

Strict mode ignores context and flags anything outside the key. The row shading shows the same thing before you place a note: green rows are chord tones, white rows fit, gray rows are outside.
